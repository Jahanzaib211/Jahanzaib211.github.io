Arabic and Urdu website development, what usually goes wrong

ALI LABS X engineering, 24 November 2026

This website is published in English, Arabic, Urdu and Chinese. Two of those read from right to left. Here is what we had to get right, and what we see going wrong elsewhere.

Turn the layout, not only the text

A right to left page is a mirror. Navigation, progress, arrows and the side that a picture sits on all change. If positions are written as left and right, every one becomes a fault. We describe them as start and end so that the browser mirrors them.

Know what must not turn

Logos, phone numbers, email addresses and product names keep their own direction. Without care, a plus sign moves to the wrong end of a phone number and nobody can call you.

Never stretch the letters

Arabic and Urdu letters join. Spacing that looks elegant in English breaks words apart. They also need taller lines, because marks sit above and below. We remove letter spacing and increase line height for both.

Choose fonts for weight

A complete Chinese font can be many megabytes. We include only the characters our pages use, which brings ours to about 200 KB. Each language loads only what it needs.

Help search engines

  • Give every language its own address.
  • Link each page to the same page in the other languages.
  • Translate titles and descriptions, not only the body.
  • Never redirect people by guessing their country. Let them choose.

Keep people where they are

Changing language should keep you on the same page. Being sent to the home page is a small insult, and it happens on many large sites.

Test with real readers

Software can find overflow and missing words. It cannot tell you that a sentence sounds foreign. Ask a native reader to use the site on a phone and tell you where they hesitate.
