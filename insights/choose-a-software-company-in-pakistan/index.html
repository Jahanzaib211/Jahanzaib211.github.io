How to choose a software company in Pakistan

ALI LABS X engineering, 24 November 2026

Pakistan has a deep pool of engineering talent and a great many software companies, from one person to thousands. We are one of them, so read this as advice from inside the field and use it on us as well.

Check that the company exists

The Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan keeps a public register where you can look up a company by name or registration number. Tax registration can be checked with the Federal Board of Revenue. A partnership or sole trader may be perfectly sound, but you should know which you are dealing with and who signs.

Look for work you can inspect

A page of client logos tells you little. Ask for a working demonstration, public source code, or a short call in which an engineer explains one hard decision and what it cost. People who did the work can talk about what went wrong.

Meet the people who will do it

Ask who will design, build and test your system, and how much of their week you will have. If the person you meet disappears after signing, ask who replaces them before you sign.

Settle ownership and support

  • Who owns the code and when.
  • Where your data is kept during and after the work.
  • How changes are priced.
  • How quickly someone answers when the system is down.
  • What a handover includes.

If you are buying from abroad

Agree the working hours that overlap, the language of documents, the currency, the governing law and the method of payment. Pay by milestone against something you can see working. Put accounts and domains in your own name from the first day.

Begin with something small

Commission one useful piece with a clear finish. A few weeks of real work shows how a team communicates, handles a mistake and keeps a date. It costs less than a wrong choice and gives you something you can use either way.

Signs to walk away

  • A firm price before any questions.
  • A promise that nothing is difficult.
  • Pressure to pay everything at the start.
  • No clear answer about who owns the result.
