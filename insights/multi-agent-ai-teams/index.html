Multi-agent AI, how to make a team of agents dependable

ALI LABS X engineering, 24 November 2026

One assistant that does everything soon loses the thread. Several agents with no structure are worse, because they agree with each other. What makes a group of people dependable also works here.

Give each agent one job

In NOVA BOTS every bot has a name, a role, instructions, a model, a memory and its own working folder. A narrow role is easier to instruct, easier to check and cheaper to run, because simple work does not need the largest model.

Let someone lead

A group has an admin that answers first and calls in specialists when they are needed. A coordinating bot called Orion can create, change and remove the others. Without a lead, agents answer everything at once and nobody finishes.

Make every handoff visible

When a bot asks another for help, the helper reports progress in the group and the one who asked returns with the result. You can see each handoff and stop any of them. A hidden handoff is where errors travel unnoticed.

Keep memory in its place

What is said in a group stays with that group. Its members use it and other chats do not. This protects privacy, and it also improves answers, since unrelated history is noise.

Separate making from checking

The agent that wrote something is a poor judge of it. Give review to a different agent with different instructions, and ask it for evidence. Two agents on the same model may still share the same blind spot, so a person decides what matters.

Put a person at the boundary

Commands that run on your computer need your approval first. Decide early which actions always require a person, and write them down. Sending, paying, deleting and granting access are a good first list.

Start with two

Begin with one agent that makes and one that checks. Add a third only when you can name a job neither should do.

This comes from our own work on NOVA BOTS.
